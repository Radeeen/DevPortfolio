## Context

Government service websites are used by people with no choice about using them
and often no familiarity with the system's internal vocabulary. A confusing
interface is not merely an inconvenience — it prevents access to a public
service.

## My role

UI/UX Designer during my internship at the Department of Communication and
Information in Tasikmalaya: user research, wireframes, prototypes, and working
with the development team through implementation.

## Approach

I began with the people rather than the screens — talking to stakeholders and
end users to find out what they came to the site to do, which is frequently not
what the site's structure assumes.

That research shaped a set of wireframes, then a responsive high-fidelity
prototype in Figma covering the main service flows. I stayed involved through
build, working with developers so that the implementation kept the structure
and spacing the design depended on.

## Result

A responsive prototype for the municipal SPBE platform, grounded in user
research rather than assumption, and delivered in a form the development team
could build from directly.

> **What I'd do differently.** The design was validated through discussion, not
> through testing — nobody attempted a task against the prototype while I
> watched. Even five moderated sessions would have told me more about where
> people got stuck than every stakeholder meeting combined, and I would insist
> on that before handoff now.
